# Food library and meal workflow

Arminius stores a compact USDA catalog in the existing Supabase database. Searches query that catalog without paid food-provider or AI calls.

## Source decision

| Source | Fit | Cost and conditions | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| USDA FoodData Central | Ingredients, prepared foods, portions, label nutrients, and GTINs in its separate data sets | Public-domain/CC0 data. Bulk downloads need no API key. The branded bulk archive is large. | Use pinned Foundation/FNDDS releases and a small, filtered branded sample. |
| Open Food Facts | Community-maintained packaged products and barcodes | ODbL attribution/share-alike terms and endpoint rate limits. Combining databases affects licensing. | Defer until barcode coverage and data boundaries are designed. |
| AI-generated nutrient numbers | Can sound plausible without matching a food or portion | Inference costs and unverifiable numbers | Do not use as the nutrition source. Future AI should propose sourced matches for review. |

References: [USDA API/licensing](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/), [USDA downloads](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/), [Open Food Facts conditions](https://support.openfoodfacts.org/help/en-gb/12-donnees-api/94-y-a-t-il-des-conditions-pour-utiliser-l-api).

## Data

After the hosted expansion, the catalog contains **6,685 sourced foods**: 5,742 generic and prepared foods (311 Foundation and 5,431 FNDDS 2021–2023 foods), 899 branded products, and 44 restaurant menu entries and components from Subway, Jimmy John's, Chick-fil-A, and Chipotle. The new USDA branded sample contains 820 grocery, bakery, beverage, and pantry products with UPCs where provided; 79 earlier catalog items remain to preserve existing food references. A clean full USDA seed contains the new 820-product sample; applying it to the hosted database preserves prior rows. Foundation and FNDDS are from USDA's April 2026 and October 2024 releases. Their source archives are pinned by URL and SHA256. Foods without complete energy and all three macros are omitted. Unknown nutrients stay absent; zero is preserved as zero.

## Restaurant and alcohol records

The restaurant menu seed contains 15 Chick-fil-A items from its [U.S. Nutrition & Allergens guide](https://www.chick-fil-a.com/nutrition-allergens), checked on September 27, 2026. The live guide does not show a publication or effective date, so the database leaves `source_release` blank rather than presenting the check date as a publisher release. Each row stores the guide serving's weight in its portion label and its complete calories, protein, carbohydrate, fat, fiber, sugar, saturated fat, sodium, cholesterol, and trans fat values where published. These are standard menu formulations; location and customization can change them. The earlier restaurant set remains one Subway item from its January 2026 U.S. guide and three Jimmy John's items from its guide effective July 22, 2024.

The next chain imports remain source-gated. McDonald's U.S. nutrition calculator says its information is correct as of January 2022, and Burger King's currently discoverable U.S. nutrition PDF is dated April 2020, so neither is represented as current menu data. Taco Bell's current official nutrition page promises full nutrient details, but its live table is not exposed in a reliable extractable format; product pages currently show calories and customization deltas without the complete panel. Wendy's U.S. ordering pages load their nutrition details dynamically, and the readily available full PDF identified in this source check is a U.K. guide, so it is not used for U.S. rows. Add these chains when a current U.S. first-party source exposes a complete item panel or table. The user's requested exclusions are Church's, Scooter's, Nothing Bundt Cakes, El Pollo Loco, Tropical Smoothie Cafe, Dutch Bros, and Dave's Hot Chicken.

The USDA FNDDS catalog already supplies generic records for brand-neutral brandy, gin, rum, tequila, vodka, and whiskey, plus regular, light, higher-alcohol, and nonalcoholic beer. Plain distilled-spirit records are source averages, not exact brand matches; proof, flavor, sweeteners, and liqueurs can change the facts. Use the generic record only when the drink matches that plain spirit category. Beer should use a style or an exact branded record because alcohol and carbohydrates vary by product. One branded example is Heineken Original Lager: the USDA branded profile stays on a 100 ml basis, while Heineken's [U.S. product page](https://www.heineken.com/us/en/our-beers/heineken-original/) confirms 5% ABV. The catalog calculates 3.95 g ethanol per 100 ml as `100 ml × 0.05 × 0.789 g/ml` and stores it under USDA nutrient ID 1018. Heineken facts were checked on September 27, 2026. A 12 fl oz serving of a 5% beer contains about 14 g ethanol, one U.S. standard drink, according to [NIAAA](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/alcohols-effects-health/what-standard-drink).

For later additions, keep each food tied to an official U.S. chain guide, manufacturer label, or USDA branded record; record the source's actual publication date when it has one. Keep omitted values unknown and never infer a macro from calories. Derive ethanol only from a clearly published ABV and entered drink volume, label the value as calculated, and do not map a brand name to a generic drink as if it were an exact brand match.

`data/usda-foundation.json` records the source URL, archive SHA256, release, license, and normalized foods. Attribution: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service, FoodData Central. Data is CC0-1.0.

Every USDA catalog food retains available nutrient values by USDA nutrient ID. Foundation and FNDDS nutrient amounts use a 100-gram basis; USDA Branded amounts use a 100-gram or 100-milliliter basis, standardized by USDA according to the supplied measure. Restaurant entries store the official nutrition panel for one complete menu item. Common calories and macros stay in typed columns; other values include calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, zinc, vitamins, cholesterol, caffeine, and fatty acids when supplied. Foundation records can have gaps; FNDDS supplies prepared foods with 65 nutrient measures. Values are never inferred to fill gaps. Branded values are label-derived and some label nutrients are absent. Raw/cooked descriptions remain explicit. USDA's [branded food documentation](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/docs/Branded_Foods_Documentation_Apr2024.pdf) explains the 100 g / 100 ml standardization.

This is a starter catalog, not complete branded-product coverage. Label entry supports foods that are missing.

## Workflow and ownership

Nutrition → Add food supports search, favorites, and recent foods. Review the labeled source serving, enter grams or milliliters for packaged foods, or choose a complete restaurant item, add up to 50 foods, then save a meal. Custom labels require calories/macros for the entire entered amount and unit; optional values can stay blank.

Recent meals displays the latest 30 entries with edit, confirmed delete, and reuse. Reuse opens a draft for review. Drafts survive navigation/reload in the current tab and are scoped to the account; closing the tab clears session storage.

The database derives new catalog entries' nutrients and stores snapshots. Portion edits and reuse scale saved snapshots, preserving provenance even after catalog changes. Custom labels are not described as USDA data. Each save is atomic; retries with the same operation ID do not duplicate meals. Stale revisions cannot overwrite or delete newer edits. RLS restricts meals and favorites to their owners.

## Free-text meal entry

For a prompt such as “100 g ground beef with half a red bell pepper, half a red onion, cooked in 10 g butter,” use the model only to extract ingredients, quantities, preparation, and unresolved details into validated JSON. Match each ingredient to this USDA catalog, show the proposed foods and portions for review, then calculate and save nutrients from the matched source records. Do not accept calories or nutrients written by the model as data.

Ask for clarification when it affects the result: ground-beef lean percentage, whether its weight is raw or cooked, and the weights for half-size produce. “Organic butter” alone is not a unique product; let the user select a catalog match or enter the label. Keep nutrients that the source omits as unknown instead of reporting zero. This makes the readout traceable and avoids false precision.

For the lowest recurring cost, test a small quantized open model in the browser with Transformers.js and WebGPU, while keeping a deterministic quantity parser as a fallback. Browser GPU support and speed depend on the device, and the first run may download model files. A model hosted on a server costs compute even when its weights are open source; set request limits and a hard usage budget before enabling that option. Local Ollama can avoid provider token charges but requires the user to run the model on their own machine, so it is better for development or an optional advanced mode than the default phone experience. See [Transformers.js WebGPU](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/guides/webgpu), [Ollama local and cloud privacy details](https://www.ollama.com/privacy), and [Ollama model hosting and pricing](https://ollama.com/blog/transparent-pricing).

Before choosing a model, compare a small set of real meal prompts on desktop and phone for ingredient extraction, quantity accuracy, latency, and download size. Pin the model version, validate its JSON against a schema, and include low-confidence cases that ask a short follow-up instead of saving a guessed meal.

## Maintenance and cost

`python scripts/import-usda.py --dataset foundation` or `--dataset fndds` downloads and normalizes the pinned archive, or accepts a local ZIP path for offline use. `python scripts/import-usda-branded.py` streams USDA's large branded archive and keeps at most eight label-complete products per selected brand term, with an overall cap of 1,200 unique products. The current curated snapshot has 820 products: 666 standardized per 100 g and 154 per 100 ml. It covers familiar brands across bakery, beverages, dairy, pantry, snacks, and sports nutrition, and includes packaged grocery products from some restaurant brands. It requires calories and all three macros and never infers missing nutrients. USDA's branded data is standardized on a 100 g or 100 ml basis, so drinks can be portioned without treating milliliters as grams. Restaurant menu data is entered separately from official chain nutrition sources; it includes one Subway item, three Jimmy John's sandwiches, and 15 Chick-fil-A entrees and sides. Subway's [January 2026 U.S. nutrition guide](https://www.subway.com/en-us/-/media/northamerica/usa/nutrition/nutritiondocuments/2026/us_nutrition_en_1-2026.pdf), Jimmy John's [nutrition guide](https://resources.jimmyjohns.com/downloadable-files/NutritionGuide.pdf), and the live Chick-fil-A guide supply the listed facts. The Chick-fil-A check date and alcohol source handling are described above. The import scripts have no database credentials or database writes. No import is scheduled and no paid data API is used.

The import scripts write local normalized files under `data/` with release, license, and archive checksum. The complete generated seed migration is ignored by Git to keep the larger data snapshot out of the repository. `node scripts/generate-usda-nutrient-migration.mjs` creates that complete seed as `supabase/migrations/202609270008_usda_catalog_seed.sql` and refreshes the tracked branded-expansion migration `supabase/migrations/202609270006_usda_branded_expansion.sql`. Existing hosted installations should apply schema migration 005, the branded expansion migration 006, and restaurant seed migration 007 in order. A new database should apply the tracked migrations in order, run the pinned USDA imports, generate the complete seed, and apply that seed after migration 007. Review each source update and create a **new seed migration** rather than rewriting an applied one. Stable IDs preserve food references, and saved meal nutrient snapshots remain fixed. A product's GTIN/UPC can be pasted into search; camera scanning is a separate UI step.

No paid food API, AI model, scheduled worker, new project, or subscription is added. Existing database and GitHub Actions usage still count against plan allowances. Full branded coverage needs a storage/freshness plan first.

## Validation and limits

Database tests execute the migrations with multiple roles, testing derived values, snapshots, invalid partial saves, stale edits, retries, pagination, and private favorites. Phone/desktop browser tests cover search, portions, favorites, retained drafts, multi-food saving, editing, reuse, and deletion against a fake backend. Live catalog checks confirm source counts and micronutrients; real-account end-to-end checks remain before public launch.

Dedicated saved recipes with serving yields and camera barcode scanning remain future work. The free-text meal plan above is still design guidance; no AI model, inference endpoint, request, or new recurring charge is connected yet.
